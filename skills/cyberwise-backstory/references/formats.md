# Formats

> **Verified:** Cyberpunk 2077 patch 2.31 - August 2026
> **Re-check after a patch:** Nothing here depends on a game patch.

**The format is usually the unlock.** "Write your backstory" is a blank page and
a performance anxiety. "Write the police file on yourself" is a game with rules,
and people who swear they cannot write will fill two pages of it.

So offer a format early - two or three, not the whole list - chosen from what
they told you the thing is *for*. Say in half a sentence why each might suit
them, and let them pick. They can change their mind later; nothing here is
expensive to redo.

The skeletons below are starting shapes, not templates to fill in. Never hand
someone a form with blanks - it turns an interview into homework.

---

## Told to someone

V telling it, out loud, to a specific person in a specific place.

**Good for:** people who freeze at "write about yourself" but can talk. Also the
best format for an unreliable narrator, because a person talking chooses what to
leave out - and what V hides from a friend is characterisation you cannot get any
other way.

**The unlock question:** *who are they telling, and why tonight?*

Shape: an opening that assumes shared knowledge (no throat-clearing), a story
that jumps to the interesting part, at least one place where V deflects, and
whatever the listener says back.

**Watch for:** it drifts into monologue. If the listener has not spoken in a
page, ask what they are doing with their face.

---

## An in-world document

The game tells most of its own story this way - files, memos, intercepted
messages, ads. Writing one is easy to start because the form does the work.

**Good for:** people who want distance from their own character, and people who
like systems. Also excellent for a V who lies, since a document has an author
with motives.

Pick whose document it is - that choice is most of the effect:

| author | what it reveals |
|---|---|
| **NCPD file** | what V has been caught doing, and what the city thinks they are |
| **Corporate personnel record** | what an employer measured, and what it missed |
| **Fixer's notes** | what V is worth, and who they can be sent against |
| **Ripperdoc's chart** | what V has done to their body and what it cost |
| **A letter that was never sent** | everything the other formats cannot say |

**The unlock question:** *who wrote this, and what do they want?*

**Watch for:** documents flatten feeling. If the whole backstory is a file, one
handwritten margin note carries more than three paragraphs of it.

---

## A braindance

The memory as a recording, with editor's annotations - what the wire caught
beyond the visuals, what is degraded, what has been cut.

**Good for:** a single formative event rather than a life. It is the most
Cyberpunk-native format there is, and it gives a reason for gaps: BD degrades,
and edits are made by someone.

**The unlock question:** *who edited it, and what did they take out?*

Shape: a timestamp, sensory layers (what V saw, felt, and the bits the editor
flags), and an interruption where the recording fails.

**Watch for:** it is a gimmick if the edit does not mean anything. The cut should
be a choice by someone with a reason.

---

## A play dossier

Not a story. A reference for *playing* the character consistently - how they
decide, what they will and will not do, where their limits sit.

**Good for:** long campaigns, roleplay across many sessions, and anyone feeding a
character into a mod or an AI companion that needs to act like them. This is the
format that gets *used* rather than read once.

A shape that works, adapted to whatever they actually care about:

- **Look** - only the details that come up in play
- **History** - short. A dossier is not a biography
- **How they decide** - the core of it. Two or three principles in their own
  words, each with the situation that proves it
- **Lines** - what they will not do, and what they will do that surprises people
- **Graded dials** - the messy human variables as levels rather than prose:
  drinking, drugs, violence, honesty, mercy. `none / low / medium / high` beats a
  paragraph, because it survives being read quickly mid-scene
- **Factions** - who they work with, who they will not, and the price of each
- **Attachments** - who can make them act against their own interest

**The unlock question:** *what would surprise someone who thinks they know V?*

**Watch for:** dials are addictive and it is easy to end up with thirty. Keep
only the ones that would actually change a decision. If a dial has never been
consulted, delete it.

---

## One scene

A single moment, written properly, instead of a chronology.

**Good for:** almost everyone, actually - and it is the best *first* format. Most
"I have no backstory" turns out to mean "I have one image and no plot". Write the
image. The rest arrives later or never, and never is fine.

**The unlock question:** *what is the moment you already picture?*

**Watch for:** nothing. This one rarely goes wrong. If they finish it and want
more, ask what happened the next morning.

---

## Interview transcript

Someone else asking V questions - a fixer vetting them, a corpo screening, a
ripperdoc taking a history, a cop with the recorder running.

**Good for:** people who like the interview you are already doing and want the
artefact to keep that shape. It also legitimises non-answers: "no comment" is a
character beat in a transcript and a hole anywhere else.

**The unlock question:** *what question do they refuse to answer?*

---

## Obituary, written early

What gets said about V after. Written now, by someone specific.

**Good for:** working out what a character is *for* when the player is stuck.
Deciding how it ends tells you what the middle has to be. Grim, effective, and
Night City's whole thesis.

**The unlock question:** *who is sorry, and who is relieved?*

---

## Choosing between them

If they cannot pick:

- They have an image, no plot → **one scene**
- They can talk but not write → **told to someone**
- They want to play the character, not read about them → **play dossier**
- They are shy about their own writing → **an in-world document**
- They are stuck on who V *is* → **obituary**
- They want one big event to matter → **braindance**

And if they want two, let them have two. A scene plus a dossier covers both
halves - the feeling and the function - and neither has to be long.
